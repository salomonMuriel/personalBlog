## The basics

I was born on 1 January 1991, which isn't great, because it's very hard to get any kind of celebration going when everyone is travelling, hungover, or both. Logically I had to do something about it, so I've celebrated my birthday on 20 April for about fifteen years now. Some years I've celebrated it on three different dates.

My parents are both Colombian and I was born and raised in Bogotá with my sister Paloma — or Paloca, as my kids call her. I'm happily married to my lifelong girlfriend: we've been together since 2010. We married in 2018 and had Franco and Luca, the super twins, in 2022. They are rambunctious, amazing, uncontrollable balls of chaos and joy. Not coincidentally, we don't have any more kids. Also, having twins is great exercise: I lost fourteen kilos since they were born, and I'm a voracious eater, especially when it comes to rice.

## Hobbies

Technology has been a lifelong passion. I never liked football — fairly rare in Colombia — and grew up glued to whatever videogame I could get my hands on. Fun fact: I learned English playing online. Since I spent so much time in front of computers, I ended up loving tech and being a fairly proud geek. Beyond gaming, I enjoy a good beer — better still in good company — making friends, travelling and a good barbecue. Most of all, I love spending time with my family, immediate and extended.

My other great passion is being a maker. I love creating new things: companies, work projects, household renovations or furniture, prototypes, cooking recipes, activities, games for the kids, you name it. I'm the kind of person with a rack full of tools and a 3D printer, constantly tinkering.

I also run, and I'm not fast. I do it because it forces me to do something hard every week, which is exactly what I ask of the people I work with. I ran a half marathon in 2:10 after not being able to do five kilometres without walking.

## Work = play

I've been lucky enough to work on my passions. Most people know me not for my tech side but because I build companies. I'm on my fifth: of the previous four, two went under and two I managed to sell.

I learned to code the hard way, with a deadline on top of me and nobody else to do it. I haven't stopped building things since.

Building companies has been a family tradition for a couple of generations. [My dad](https://www.linkedin.com/in/raul-h-muriel-b-3904b62/) is a successful entrepreneur — still starting new ventures — and his father before him too. It's the only thing we've ever known how to do.

Today I build [Ignia](https://www.ignia.lat?utm_source=salomon-personal-site), a new model for higher education, Monday to Friday. Alongside it I run this personal practice, which is in my own name and separate: I build custom software for Colombian companies still doing by hand what could already run on its own.
